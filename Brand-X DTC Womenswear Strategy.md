# Brand X: Building a DTC Womenswear Brand from Scratch

**End-to-end strategy and budget framework, from design to customer**

Author: Samet Bakar (Supply Chain & Demand Planning) | September 2026

> **Note:** This is an anonymized case study. The brand name, website, social handles, product names and discount codes have been removed. All figures are either public-level observations or sector-average assumptions. They are not the brand's internal data. Wherever a number is an assumption, it is marked as such.

---

## Context

Brand X is a Turkish contemporary womenswear brand selling dresses, jumpsuits, trousers, shirts, suits, shorts and vests. Observations from its public website:

- Small catalog (under 100 SKUs), sold only through its own online store (DTC). No physical store found.
- Price range roughly 3,500 to 12,000 TL (dresses 4,900 to 7,500 TL, suits up to 12,000 TL).
- Mixed size labelling (S/M/L and 36/38/40). Stock is deepest in S and M, then L, with XS and XL shallow.
- Fabric language: crepe, chiffon, satin, viscose. Fluid, body-fitting silhouettes.
- Positioning around "inner strength" and individuality.
- A first-order discount code and a site-wide 20% coupon were both active. This is a strategic issue in itself (see 1.2).

---

## 1. Pricing Strategy

### 1.1 Price architecture

The 3,500 to 12,000 TL range should be split into three tiers instead of pricing each item on its own.

| Tier | Price band | Role | Example categories |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | 3,500 – 4,900 TL | Low-risk first purchase for new customers | Shorts, basic tops, plain mini dress |
| Core (NOS) | 4,900 – 6,500 TL | Volume engine, always in stock | Halter dress, straight trousers, shirts |
| Statement (Hero) | 6,500 – 12,000 TL | Lifts brand perception, low volume, high margin | Suits, patterned or textured dresses, jumpsuits |

Each tier should hold a defined share of the collection. This spreads stock risk and avoids a "everything is on sale" image.

### 1.2 Should discounts be used?

Yes, but tiered and rule-based, not a blanket coupon. A site-wide 20% coupon weakens a premium positioning and signals that the list price is inflated.

- **Entry:** 20 to 30% discount is acceptable for end-of-season and stock clearance.
- **Core:** Excluded from discounts. This is the margin engine.
- **Statement:** Only limited invite-only offers (e.g. VIP early access). Never a general coupon.

### 1.3 Campaigns: where do customers come from?

Treat campaigns as demand triggers, not price cuts.

- **Season transitions:** Clear previous-season Entry and Core stock (SS to FW). Discounts make sense here.
- **New collection launches:** Use scarcity (e.g. 48-hour early access) instead of discounts.
- **List building:** Turn the first-order code into a "join the community" tool to collect CRM data (email/SMS).
- **Micro-influencer and UGC:** The brand voice suits personal storytelling better than corporate ads.

*Data needed:* the real split of customer acquisition between organic Instagram, paid ads and word of mouth. Until then, a sector-average split is used.

### 1.4 Mark-up on NOS products

Standard practice is a multiplier on production cost (fabric + labor + trims).

| Product type | Suggested mark-up | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| NOS / Core | 2.6x – 3.2x | Reliable demand, low stock risk, volume-based |
| Seasonal assortment / fashion | 3.3x – 4.0x | Uncertain demand, higher markdown risk |
| Statement / hero | 3.8x – 4.5x | Low units, high perceived value, no discounts |

*Note:* These ranges reflect general contemporary-segment practice. Real cost breakdowns (fabric meterage and price, CMT labor, trims) are needed to convert them into TL figures.

## 2. Customer Base and Persona

### 2.1 Primary persona: "The Visible, Conscious Woman"

Derived from price band, brand language and silhouette choices (assumption, to be validated with real order data):

- **Age:** 26 to 38 (core 28 to 34)
- **Lifestyle:** Urban, career-oriented, attends social events often
- **Spending:** Upper-middle income, selective, willing to spend 5,000+ TL on a single item but not buying every month
- **Use case:** Occasion wear (evening events, birthdays, holidays), not everyday basics
- **Purchase trigger:** Outfits seen on Instagram, influencer posts, friend recommendations

A second persona (e.g. 22 to 26, trend-driven) could be tested later, but focusing on one persona first keeps the brand clear.

### 2.2 Size distribution and production weights

Observed stock depth by size: S and M deepest, L medium, XS low, XL lowest. This reflects stock held, not sales speed.

| Size | Observed stock depth | Suggested production share |
|---|---|---|
| S / 36 and M / 38 | Deepest (core sizes) | 60 – 65% combined |
| L / 40 | Medium | 20 – 25% |
| XS / 34 and XL | Low | 10 – 15% combined |

*Note:* A size that looks deep in stock may simply be selling slowly. Real size-level sell-through data is needed to correct this split.

## 3. Product Features and Cost Items

### 3.1 Critical design points

- **Pattern consistency:** Body-fitting design has the least tolerance for pattern errors. The gap between size chart and real body measurements drives return rates directly.
- **Fabric drape test:** Test fluid fabrics (crepe, chiffon, satin) on a moving model (walking, sitting), not only in static photos.
- **Dye-lot consistency:** Saturated colors often vary between batches. Define an acceptable tolerance in advance.
- **Lining and inner construction:** Matters for comfort and a premium feel, but it is usually the first item cut from the budget.

### 3.2 Peer positioning

Brand X competes in Turkey's contemporary/premium womenswear segment, not in the mass market. Brands in this segment typically:

- Use small, frequent "drop" capsules.
- Combine physical stores, strong e-commerce and selected multi-brand retailers.
- Mix studio shoots with real-life or street-style content.

*(Peer pricing and campaign practices were not verified here. A live comparison would be a separate step.)*

### 3.3 Fabric composition

| Fabric | Suggested content | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Crepe | 95% polyester / 5% elastane (or 100% viscose crepe for premium) | Dresses, trousers |
| Chiffon | 100% polyester (viscose lining) | Long dresses, jumpsuits |
| Satin | 97% polyester / 3% elastane | Shirts, sets |
| Knit / body-fitting | 90 to 95% main yarn / 5 to 10% elastane | Halter dresses, bodysuit-style tops |

*Note:* These are general price-band suggestions. A sustainability or organic positioning would change fabric choice and cost completely.

### 3.4 Basic vs. design balance

Suggested split: **35% basic** (NOS, plain, simple cut) and **65% design** (pattern, detail, limited quantity). Mass-market brands usually run the opposite (60 to 70% basic), but this positioning does not justify that.

*Note:* The split depends on the initial production budget. With a small budget, raising the basic share lowers risk.

### 3.5 Cost structure (sector averages)

**Within production cost (COGS)**

| Item | Average share |
|---|---|
| Fabric | 35 – 45% |
| Cut and sew (CMT labor) | 25 – 35% |
| Trims (buttons, zippers, labels) | 5 – 8% |
| Pattern and samples (amortized) | 3 – 6% |
| Packaging | 2 – 4% |
| Quality control and inbound logistics | 3 – 5% |

**As a share of retail price**

| Item | Average share |
|---|---|
| Production cost (COGS) | 25 – 35% |
| Marketing (Meta, Google, influencer) | 10 – 15% |
| Marketplace commission (if used) | 15 – 25% |
| Shipping and returns | 5 – 8% |
| Overhead / fixed costs | 8 – 12% |
| Target net margin | 10 – 18% |

*Note:* These are sector benchmarks, a skeleton to be replaced with real supplier quotes, shipping agreements and ad spend.

## 4. Sales Channels

### 4.1 E-commerce channels

| Channel | Fit | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Own website | High | Full brand control, highest margin. Should remain the main channel |
| Instagram / TikTok Shop | High | Visual brand, direct conversion |
| Premium multi-brand platform | Medium-High | Raises brand perception, but commission is high |
| General marketplaces | Low-Medium | Brings volume, but discount culture and 15 to 25% commission hurt margin and image |
| International marketplaces | Long-term option | Only with an international growth goal. High operational complexity |

### 4.2 Consignment

Possible, and reasonable as an entry strategy for a new brand.

- **Pros:** Easier for boutiques to accept (they carry no stock risk).
- **Cons:** Cash returns late, stock control is harder, pricing and discount decisions can shift to the store.
- **Best use:** Testing a new city or region with selected concept stores.

### 4.3 Recommended channel priority

1. Own site + Instagram/TikTok Shop
2. Selected premium multi-brand platform
3. Consignment with selected concept stores
4. General marketplaces, only for stock clearance, never as a main channel

## 5. Points Often Overlooked

### 5.1 Feasibility and break-even

One question must be answered first: how many units must be sold to cover fixed costs? Required inputs: monthly fixed costs, net profit per unit, and average order value (AOV).

### 5.2 Stock-based vs. order-based production

- **NOS items:** Fast reorder from stocked fabric (2 to 4 weeks, driven by demand signals).
- **Statement items:** One-time order production with low quantities and a narrow size range to limit leftover risk.

### 5.3 Assortment vs. NOS decision matrix

| Criterion | Assortment (seasonal) | NOS (continuous) |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast confidence | Low | High |
| Leftover stock risk | High | Low |
| Margin target | High (covers the risk) | Medium (volume compensates) |
| Example | Patterned or special items | Repeated core silhouettes |

### 5.4 KPIs

- **Sell-through rate:** at 4 weeks, 8 weeks and season end, by product and size
- **Full-price sales ratio:** the clearest indicator of brand health
- **Return rate:** by product and size, to catch pattern issues early
- **Inventory turnover:** tracked separately for NOS and assortment
- **CAC vs. LTV:** customer acquisition cost against lifetime value

### 5.5 Supply chain and capacity

- Test workshop capacity for peak periods (pre-holiday, year-end) in advance.
- Avoid single-supplier dependence for signature fabrics. Keep at least one backup.
- Plan sample-to-production lead time backwards from the season calendar.

### 5.6 Brand risks

- **Price-value conflict:** High prices combined with a permanent 20% coupon do not work together. One of them has to change.
- **Single-channel dependence:** Reliance on one site and one social platform exposes sales to algorithm changes.
- **Limited size range:** If the range stops at XL, is that a deliberate positioning or a missed market? This should be a conscious decision.

To turn this framework into a numbers-based plan, the following inputs are required:

3. Customer acquisition split (organic / paid / referral)
4. Real production costs (fabric, labor, trims)
5. Order data to validate the persona
6. Size-level sales speed
7. Peer price and campaign benchmarks
8. Actual fabric compositions and any sustainability goal
9. Initial production budget (TL)

13. Monthly fixed cost estimate
14. Number and location of production partners
15. Decision on extending the size range beyond XL
